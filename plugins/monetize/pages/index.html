How web games earn

A browser game earns from the platform it is played on. The platform owns the players, the ads and the payments, and it pays you a share. Your part is to fit its rules and give it good moments to show an ad.

The ways money comes in

ModelHow it worksWhere you see it
Ad revenue shareThe platform shows ads in your game through its own SDK and pays part of what they earn. The most common model on web portals.Poki, CrazyGames, Yandex Games, GameDistribution, YouTube Playables, Playgama
Your own adsOn a site you host, you place ads yourself through an ad network made for HTML5 games and keep the publisher share.Your own domain with Google AdSense H5 Games Ads, a Telegram Mini App with a Mini App ad network
In-game purchasesPlayers buy items through the platform's payment system. Many portals don't allow it, or allow it only for invited games.Yandex Games, Facebook Instant Games, Playgama, CrazyGames by invitation
Paid game or paid contentPlayers pay for the game or unlock part of it. The store keeps a fee.itch.io, Wavedash
LicensingA portal or publisher pays you for the game itself, sometimes for exclusivity. Agreed per game, not through an SDK.Portals that pick games by hand

What decides how much a game earns

  • Playtime and return visits. Ads are sold per impression, so minutes played and players coming back count for more than anything else.
  • Where the players are. Advertisers pay very different prices by country and device.
  • Good ad moments. Natural breaks the player accepts, and rewarded ads players choose because the reward is worth it.
  • Load time. Players leave before a slow game starts. A small first download keeps them.
  • Fit with the platform. Curated portals promote games that follow their integration rules and test well with their players.

The one rule every portal shares

A portal allows only its own ads, called through its own SDK. No other ad network, no ad images of your own, and usually no links out. So a game carries a different ad integration on each platform. Either build a version per platform with that platform's SDK, or use one cross-platform layer, such as the Playgama Bridge SDK, that calls the right SDK on each platform.

The events every SDK asks for

The names differ, the moments don't:

MomentWhat you tell the SDK
The game finished loadingLoading is done and the player can start.
Play starts or resumesGameplay started, on the player's first input and after every pause, menu or level end.
Play stopsGameplay stopped: pause, menu, level end, game over, cutscene.
A natural breakA place for a full-screen ad. The platform decides whether one actually shows.
The player asks for a rewardShow a rewarded ad and say whether the player earned the reward.
