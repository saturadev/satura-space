Reading a trend

A trend worth acting on is a format many people repeat, not one lucky video. These are the signals the agent weighs, and what makes a trend turn into a game people want to play.

Signals

SignalWhy it matters
Speed, not sizeViews per hour since posting. A young video climbing fast says more about now than an old one with millions.
Repeated by manyThe same setup, sound or hashtag across different creators means a format, not a fluke.
Rising soundsA sound used in more and more new videos is often the earliest sign of a trend.
Saves and sharesSaves and shares per view show what people want to come back to or send to friends.
Comments asking for more"Where can I play this", "part 2", "how do I do this" mark demand.
Small creators doing wellWhen a format works for accounts without a big following, it works because of the format.

What makes a trend a game

  • One clear action you can turn into a control: stack, dodge, cut, drop, aim, sort.
  • Tension or satisfaction in a few seconds: a near miss, a perfect fit, a chain reaction, a fail.
  • A camera you can copy: side view, top-down, first person, the same framing in every video.
  • Room for a score: faster, more, longer, perfect.
  • Your own take. Keep the mechanic and the mood. Names, characters, art and music are your own, never another game's or a creator's.

What to skip

  • Trends that depend on a person's face, voice or a celebrity.
  • Videos showing another game: take the idea, never its content.
  • Anything a platform's rules or your audience's age group rule out.
